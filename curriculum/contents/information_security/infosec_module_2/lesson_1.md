# Introduction to Risk

## What Is Risk?

In information security, **risk** is the potential for loss or harm resulting from a threat exploiting a vulnerability. Understanding risk is the foundation of every security decision you will ever make.

> "You can't protect everything. You have to know what matters, what threatens it, and how likely that threat is."

---

## The Risk Equation

Risk is often expressed as a simple relationship:

```
Risk = Likelihood × Impact
```

- **Likelihood** — How probable is it that a threat will exploit a vulnerability?
- **Impact** — How severe would the consequences be if it did?

A low-likelihood, low-impact risk (e.g., a spider on your server room door) is very different from a high-likelihood, high-impact risk (e.g., unpatched public-facing systems).

---

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Threat** | Any potential cause of an unwanted incident (attacker, natural disaster, human error) |
| **Vulnerability** | A weakness that a threat can exploit (unpatched software, weak password policy) |
| **Risk** | The intersection of threat and vulnerability, weighted by impact |
| **Asset** | Anything of value that needs protection (data, systems, reputation) |

---

## Why Risk Management Matters

Organizations have finite budgets and cannot eliminate every vulnerability. Risk management gives a structured way to:

1. Identify what assets are at stake
2. Understand what threatens those assets
3. Prioritize effort based on likelihood and impact
4. Make conscious decisions about what risk to accept
